How to read this report
Hit@k — was the evidence found?

The share of labeled queries whose answer evidence was covered by the final top-k chunks. Higher is better.

Answer rank (MRR) — how early?

Rewards placing the complete answer near the top. 1.00 means it was always complete at rank 1.

95% confidence range — how certain?

The plausible range around hit@k from this query sample. More labeled queries usually make it narrower.

Fragile hit — worked across pieces

No single chunk covered enough evidence; several returned chunks did so together. Small chunking changes may break it.

Candidates vs. top-k

Candidates are the larger shortlist retrieved first. Top-k is the smaller final set delivered after fusion or reranking.

Token budget — context limit

An optional cap on whole retrieved chunks sent onward. It compares configs at a similar context cost.

Where a missed answer was first lost

Each miss is counted once, at the earliest pipeline stage that could no longer cover the labeled answer evidence.

Representation — the answer text isn't in any chunk — ingestion lost it ANN approximation — exact dense search found it, but the HNSW approximation did not Not retrieved — neither dense nor sparse fetched it into the candidates Fusion drop — candidates had it, but combining dense + sparse lost it Reranker demotion — the reranker reordered it out of the top-k Final cutoff — retrieved and ranked, but it landed just past the top-k Budget cutoff — it was in the top-k, but the token budget dropped it

Quality vs. context trade-offs (the Pareto frontier)

“Pareto frontier” is the standard name for the outer edge of best trade-offs: a point is on it when no other tested config is both more accurate and uses the same or fewer retrieved tokens. There may be several good choices because improving quality can require more context.
